Research focus
Xu et al. (2025) investigate how different ways of integrating AI into design workflows affect creative outcomes, cognitive activities and user perceptions. Their FigJam plugin supports two exercises: Five Whys and Competitive Analysis (PDF pp. 2, 4).

Method
The study analysed 47 participants randomly assigned to No-AI, Co-led or AI-led conditions. Co-led support provided incremental interaction, while AI-led support populated the templates in advance. The researchers analysed participants’ written outcomes, screen recordings, questionnaires and interviews (PDF pp. 5–8).

Key findings
Both AI conditions supported broader problem framing. After competitive analysis, AI-led participants produced final solutions covering more topics. They spent more time reviewing and synthesising information, while No-AI and Co-led participants engaged more in writing and revision. No-AI and Co-led participants also reported greater cognitive engagement and rated their initial solutions as more creative (PDF pp. 8–11).

Critical assessment
Topic diversity is only one aspect of creative outcomes. The study inferred reflection time from observable behaviour, which cannot fully reveal the quality of thinking. Its short tasks and mainly novice participants also limit transfer to long-term creative practice. The authors acknowledge that preset prompts could steer participants towards technological solutions (PDF pp. 7–8, 13).

Implications for ProjectOS
I propose exploring source-linked suggestions that users can question, revise or reject. Reflective prompts should relate to the user’s project and priorities. Whether this approach supports understanding without adding excessive effort remains a question for evaluation.

Xu, X. (Tone), Konnova, A., Gao, B., Peng, C., Vo, D. and Dow, S.P. (2025) ‘Productive vs. Reflective: How Different Ways of Integrating AI into Design Workflows Affect Cognition and Motivation’, Proceedings of the 2025 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems.
