Reading Notes — Reflective AI (2026)

Reference

van der Burg, V., et al. (2026). Reflective AI: A Slow Technology Approach for Design Education. Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, Article 89, 1–19. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791691

Research focus

The paper explores how AI can support reflection on creative practice through deliberate engagement with data, annotation, and interpretation. Its concept of Reflective AI concerns human reflection, rather than an AI system reflecting on its own reasoning.

Method

The authors conducted a three-day Research-through-Design workshop with eleven design students. Participants collected personally meaningful images, developed subjective labels, annotated datasets, and created artworks for interpretation by customized YOLOv5 object detection models. The analysis drew on creative artifacts, recorded discussions, written reflections, and tutor notes.

Main findings

Label selection helped participants articulate aesthetic judgments, while anticipating model interpretations influenced their creative decisions. Unexpected predictions prompted both symbolic interpretations of their work and insights into machine learning. The authors distinguish learning through AI from learning about AI.

Relevance to ProjectOS

The study suggests investigating reflection during material selection and interpretation, rather than only after an AI response. ProjectOS could allow users to inspect retrieved sources, identify outdated information, and revise the system’s interpretation. These are proposed applications to RAG, not interactions evaluated in this study.

Critical limitations

The small, short workshop and absence of a comparison condition limit causal and long-term claims. Tutor guidance, making activities, and group discussion may also have contributed to reflection. Furthermore, interpretive ambiguity in an artistic exercise does not justify factual errors in document-grounded assistance.
