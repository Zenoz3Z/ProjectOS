Reading Notes — Dalsgaard, Biskjaer and Frich (2023)

Reference

Dalsgaard, P., Biskjaer, M. M., and Frich, J. (2023). Capturing and revisiting ideas in the design process: A longitudinal technology probe study. Design Studies, 88, 101200. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2023.101200

Research problem

Creative professionals use many tools to capture ideas, but often lack structured routines for revisiting them. The study investigates professional designers’ idea-management practices and the implications of introducing a digital tool into these practices.

Method

The authors deployed Recollect, a lightweight mobile technology probe, with ten professional designers for approximately one month. Forty recurring interviews were conducted and analysed thematically. The study aimed to generate insights into practice rather than demonstrate that Recollect was superior to existing tools.

Main findings

Participants valued capturing and revisiting ideas, but active revisiting remained uncommon. Although they often understood ideas as solutions or partial solutions, their workflows, formats, routines, and sharing practices were highly diverse. Participants wanted resurfacing, overview, search, version distinction, and integration with existing tools.

Relevance to ProjectOS

The findings support focusing ProjectOS on project re-entry and the contextual resurfacing of previous materials. RAG could retrieve earlier decisions, feedback, and unresolved ideas in relation to a current question. Source, date, and version information may help users interpret the retrieved material. These are design hypotheses rather than outcomes demonstrated by the paper.

Critical limitations

The study involved a small and relatively narrow professional design sample. Repeated interviews may themselves have encouraged reflection, and the results rely mainly on qualitative accounts. The paper does not evaluate RAG, creative students, or the quality of creative outcomes.
