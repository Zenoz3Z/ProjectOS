Research focus
Tankelevitch et al. (2024) use metacognition to explain usability challenges in generative AI. Metacognition involves monitoring and controlling one’s own thinking, including awareness of goals, confidence in judgment, task decomposition and adjustment of strategies (PDF pp. 2–4).

Type of contribution
This is a conceptual paper drawing on psychology, cognitive science and existing AI user studies. It does not report a new participant experiment. It proposes a framework, design opportunities and questions for future research (PDF pp. 1–2).

Main argument
Working with generative AI requires users to formulate and revise prompts, evaluate outputs, and decide whether and how AI should fit into their workflows. Delegating work therefore still requires judgment about goals, output quality and appropriate reliance (PDF pp. 5–11).

Design opportunities
The authors propose supporting users through planning, self-evaluation and self-management, alongside reducing system demands through explainability and appropriate customizability. They also caution that additional prompts and explanations can increase cognitive load (PDF pp. 12–18).

Relevance to ProjectOS
This framework motivates investigating whether project records and source-linked responses help creators understand and assess AI suggestions. Optional reflection support may be useful, but its timing and effort require evaluation. These are design hypotheses; the paper does not establish the effectiveness of RAG for long-term creative projects.

Critical assessment
The proposed interventions require further empirical testing. Evidence from programming, education and other settings may not transfer directly to creative practice. Increased confidence alone would also be insufficient evidence of better judgment.

Tankelevitch, L., Kewenig, V., Simkute, A., Scott, A.E., Sarkar, A., Sellen, A. and Rintel, S. (2024) ‘The Metacognitive Demands and Opportunities of Generative AI’, Proceedings of the CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems.
