# Initial analysis codebook v0

Status: **Draft for supervisor review — existing ethics approval unchanged; data-storage wording updated following supervisor feedback on 9 October 2026.** Blank public master.

This is an initial **deductive codebook** for source visibility, user correction and project re-entry. It defines starting concepts, not study findings. Round 1 may add inductive codes: record their definition, reason and version in private analysis notes. A passage may receive more than one code. See [template guidance](README.md).

Do not interpret `Update` automatically as system failure or `Confirm` automatically as objective correctness. Participant-assessed alignment is not ground truth. An evidence opening alone cannot establish verification, trust or understanding. Review actions and ratings need contextual explanation before interpretive coding.

Preserve negative cases, uncertainty and inconsistencies within and across accounts before deciding themes. Distinguish participant statements, manual observations, saved reviews and researcher interpretation. P96 is excluded from formal analysis. Use de-identified references in private memos; leave all public memos blank. No participant examples are supplied or invented here.

## 1. perceived_alignment

- **Code name:** `perceived_alignment`
- **Working definition:** The participant's expressed sense of how the reconstruction fits their current understanding.
- **Include when:** The participant describes a fit, partial fit or mismatch and what informed that judgement.
- **Exclude when:** Only a saved Confirm/Update/Reject or numeric rating is available without explanation; an analyst asserts objective accuracy.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview explanations, rating comments, saved correction reasons, comparison of the pre-test account with the participant's later explanation.

## 2. historical_current_confusion

- **Code name:** `historical_current_confusion`
- **Working definition:** An expressed difficulty distinguishing earlier, abandoned or resolved states from what is current.
- **Include when:** The participant identifies temporal misattribution or describes uncertainty about whether a direction, problem or action still applies.
- **Exclude when:** A historical detail is recognised as historical and causes no reported confusion; a date is absent without any stated temporal consequence.
- **Possible data sources:** Correction reasons, interview notes, explicit statements recorded during review.
- **Memo:** __________

## 3. missing_or_insufficient_evidence

- **Code name:** `missing_or_insufficient_evidence`
- **Working definition:** Evidence is reported missing or insufficient for judging a reconstruction, including responses to an absence indicator.
- **Include when:** The participant describes missing support, an absent perspective or how an insufficient-evidence message affected judgement.
- **Exclude when:** Evidence is available but difficult to use for a different reason; the researcher equates absence from a selected pack with absence from the real project.
- **Possible data sources:** Rating comments, interview notes, saved reasons, manual observations of explicitly discussed absence.
- **Memo:** __________

## 4. evidence_supported_verification

- **Code name:** `evidence_supported_verification`
- **Working definition:** Visible source excerpts help the participant check, qualify or reject a summary.
- **Include when:** The participant explains how an excerpt informed a judgement, including finding a mismatch.
- **Exclude when:** An excerpt was merely opened, or usefulness is assumed by the researcher without an explanation.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview notes, source-visibility comments, review reasons, explicitly stated checks in observation notes.
- **Memo:** __________

## 5. evidence_did_not_help

- **Code name:** `evidence_did_not_help`
- **Working definition:** Available excerpts are described as unhelpful for assessing a summary.
- **Include when:** The participant describes irrelevant, fragmented, confusing or insufficiently contextualised excerpts as limiting a judgement.
- **Exclude when:** The only issue is that no evidence exists in the interface; excerpts were not opened and there is no explanation of their usefulness.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview notes, rating comments, review reasons, explicit statements during evidence inspection.
- **Memo:** __________

## 6. correction_restored_control

- **Code name:** `correction_restored_control`
- **Working definition:** Reviewing or correcting a reconstruction is described as helping the participant express their own account or regain control.
- **Include when:** The participant connects Confirm/Update/Reject or correction text with agency, ownership or ability to challenge the reconstruction.
- **Exclude when:** A correction was entered without an explanation of control; the researcher assumes any editable field creates agency.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview notes, correction/control comments, saved reasons with relevant explanation.
- **Memo:** __________

## 7. correction_or_review_burden

- **Code name:** `correction_or_review_burden`
- **Working definition:** Effort, time or friction associated with checking evidence, deciding an action or writing a correction.
- **Include when:** The participant describes excessive or acceptable effort, or an observable repeated action is recorded with its context and without inferring mental state.
- **Exclude when:** A pause or task duration alone is treated as proof of burden; an unrelated technical interruption has no documented review consequence.
- **Possible data sources:** Verification-burden comments, interview notes, contextual manual observations and assistance records.
- **Memo:** __________

## 8. trust_calibration

- **Code name:** `trust_calibration`
- **Working definition:** The participant describes adjusting reliance on the reconstruction in response to evidence, absence, correction or uncertainty.
- **Include when:** The participant explains why they accepted, questioned, checked or limited reliance on a claim.
- **Exclude when:** General approval, a Confirm action or an evidence opening is treated as trust without an explanation.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview explanations, saved review reasons, rating comments, explicitly stated uncertainty.
- **Memo:** __________

## 9. project_reentry_value

- **Code name:** `project_reentry_value`
- **Working definition:** Reported usefulness or lack of usefulness for recalling, clarifying or resuming a paused project.
- **Include when:** The participant connects the process with remembering context, identifying priorities, considering next steps or finding no re-entry benefit.
- **Exclude when:** General interface preference has no connection to returning to a project; a future productivity gain is asserted without evidence.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview notes, re-entry rating comments, optional reflections and later explanations of the pre-test account.
- **Memo:** __________

## 10. interface_comprehension_problem

- **Code name:** `interface_comprehension_problem`
- **Working definition:** Difficulty understanding interface wording, evidence status, controls or the review/navigation process.
- **Include when:** The participant asks what a control/status means, describes unclear wording, or an observable misunderstanding is documented with context.
- **Exclude when:** A technical fault occurs without a comprehension issue; disagreement concerns only the reconstructed project content.
- **Possible data sources:** Help requests, neutral observation notes, interview notes and clarity/absence comments.
- **Memo:** __________

## 11. schema_did_not_fit_project

- **Code name:** `schema_did_not_fit_project`
- **Working definition:** The participant describes how the six-part structure fails to represent an aspect of their creative project.
- **Include when:** A section is reported as inapplicable, an important dimension is missing, or the structure imposes an unsuitable interpretation.
- **Exclude when:** A summary is disputed within an otherwise suitable section; a missing excerpt alone is assumed to prove a schema problem.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview explanations, correction reasons and optional reflections.
- **Memo:** __________

## 12. misleading_reconstruction

- **Code name:** `misleading_reconstruction`
- **Working definition:** A reconstruction is described as encouraging an unsupported or distorted understanding of the project.
- **Include when:** The participant identifies misleading attribution, unsupported certainty or a consequential mistaken impression; retain any uncertainty in the account.
- **Exclude when:** A minor preference, Update or Reject is automatically labelled misleading without context.
- **Possible data sources:** Saved reasons, interview notes, explicit critical-incident statements and associated manual observations.
- **Memo:** __________

## 13. requested_feature_or_change

- **Code name:** `requested_feature_or_change`
- **Working definition:** An explicit suggestion for changing the tool or supervised study process.
- **Include when:** The participant requests a capability, wording, structure or workflow change and, where supplied, explains the need.
- **Exclude when:** The researcher invents a solution or treats a complaint as an explicit feature request.
- **Possible data sources:** Interview notes, the one-change reflection, rating comments and explicitly stated requests during the task.

## Analytic memo prompts for private use

Track code revisions, ambiguous or uncoded passages, co-occurring codes, counterexamples and differences between cases. Keep descriptive notes distinct from interpretations and tentative design implications. A theme requires a documented interpretive argument, not a count of codes alone. Leave the matrix's theme fields blank until there is supporting material, and do not present this codebook as results.
