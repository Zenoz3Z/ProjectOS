# Iteration 1 Research Questions

## Research context

ProjectOS is a source-grounded and correctable project re-entry tool for creative students and practitioners working on long-term projects.

It generates a six-part Project Re-entry Brief from project records. The brief presents the current intention, important changes, current and superseded directions, relevant feedback, unresolved tensions and a possible next action.

The generated reconstruction remains provisional. Users can inspect the supporting evidence and confirm, correct or reject the system’s interpretation.

## Primary research question

How can a source-grounded and correctable Project Re-entry Brief support creative students in reconstructing project state and deciding what to do next after an interruption?

## Secondary research questions

### RQ1 — Comprehension

How easily can users understand the current state of a creative project from the generated Re-entry Brief?

This includes whether users can identify:

- the project’s current intention;
- important changes and their reasons;
- current and superseded directions;
- relevant tutor or user feedback;
- unresolved tensions;
- the next documented action.

### RQ2 — Source grounding

How does access to visible source evidence affect users’ interpretation of and trust in the generated summaries?

This includes whether users:

- inspect the supporting evidence;
- understand where a summary came from;
- notice when evidence is historical or superseded;
- avoid treating the AI-generated summary as automatically correct.

### RQ3 — Correctability and control

How do the review and correction controls affect users’ sense of control over the reconstructed project state?

This includes whether users understand and use:

- Confirm;
- Update this summary;
- Reject;
- Mark as superseded.

### RQ4 — Reflection and next action

Does the Re-entry Brief help users reflect on the project and identify a meaningful next action?

The study will examine whether the suggested next action appears:

- relevant to the documented project state;
- understandable;
- actionable;
- appropriately provisional.

### RQ5 — Limitations and cognitive effort

Where does ProjectOS create confusion, additional work or misleading interpretations?

Particular attention will be given to:

- summaries that combine current and historical information;
- plausible but unsupported statements;
- unclear project-state labels;
- excessive evidence or interface detail;
- corrections that require too much effort;
- uncertainty about what to do next.

## Purpose of Iteration 1

Iteration 1 is an exploratory usability and perception study.

It is not intended to prove that ProjectOS improves creativity or productivity. It will investigate how participants understand, inspect and correct a source-grounded reconstruction of a creative project.

The findings will be used to improve the second prototype iteration.

## Participants

The study will involve approximately five to eight adult participants.

Participants should be:

- creative students or creative practitioners;
- involved in projects that continue over multiple sessions;
- familiar with project notes, feedback, design decisions or process documentation;
- able to provide informed consent.

Participants do not need previous experience with RAG or AI development.

## Test material

Iteration 1 will use the non-sensitive `sample_echo_field` demonstration dataset.

Participants will not be required to upload their own project documents during this iteration.

Using the same demonstration project will allow the study to examine interface comprehension and review behaviour without exposing private participant material or introducing major differences between individual project datasets.

## Participant tasks

Participants will be asked to:

1. open the ProjectOS Re-entry Brief;
2. generate the project summary;
3. identify the project’s current intention;
4. identify one important change and its reason;
5. distinguish a current direction from a superseded direction;
6. inspect the source evidence for at least one section;
7. identify the unresolved tensions;
8. identify the proposed next action;
9. confirm, update or reject at least one reconstruction;
10. explain whether the brief would help them return to one of their own long-term creative projects.

## Data to collect

The study may collect:

- task completion observations;
- areas where participants hesitate or request help;
- whether supporting evidence is opened;
- whether review controls are understood;
- corrections or rejections made during the task;
- short rating-scale responses;
- semi-structured interview responses;
- researcher observation notes.

No biometric, medical or highly sensitive personal data will be collected.

## Evaluation focus

The evaluation will focus on:

- clarity;
- perceived usefulness;
- perceived control;
- source transparency;
- trust calibration;
- reflective value;
- ease of identifying the next action;
- misunderstandings and limitations.

The evaluation will not treat semantic similarity scores as factual confidence.

## Expected contribution

The study is expected to show whether ProjectOS provides value beyond general AI summarisation by combining:

- project-state reconstruction;
- visible supporting evidence;
- distinctions between current, historical, superseded and future information;
- explicit uncertainty;
- user review and correction;
- persistent user-authored interpretations.

## Current status

This research design is provisional. It will be refined after reviewing the participant information sheet, consent form, task script and interview questions.
