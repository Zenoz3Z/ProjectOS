## Preliminary themes

- **Completed-project states:** Not applicable provided a response when a documented state was not recognised as currently active or relevant. Both projects were described as complete or largely complete, limiting transfer to paused but active projects.
- **Interface comprehension:** No interface, navigation, terminology or lag problems were reported or recorded in these two sessions. Almost no researcher explanation was required. These observations do not establish a causal improvement over the earlier iteration.
- **Source visibility and checking:** Supporting evidence was available, but opening was observed in only two of twelve sections. Availability and perceived usefulness of sources do not establish that verification occurred.
- **Past and present state:** Historical records can remain relevant to a project's development while no longer representing a participant-recognised current aim, issue or task. Not applicable does not automatically mean that a source was absent.
- **Appropriate responses and incomplete selection:** A response that fits a completed-project perspective can coexist with incomplete evidence selection. Earlier preparation diagnostics support keeping those possibilities distinct; participant acceptance or non-applicability does not validate retrieval.
- **Content coverage and usefulness:** Few populated summaries and generic insufficient-evidence responses affected perceived usefulness in the supplied account. A request for clearer indications of missing information suggests a direction for further investigation; that request was explicitly recorded for one participant.
- **Changes and reasons:** The section on changes and reasons was considered reasonably accurate in both sessions and was identified as especially helpful in one. This is participant-assessed alignment, not objective accuracy.

## Limits and preliminary conclusion

The evidence comes from only two participants, researcher-organised source packs, retrospective self-report and manual observations. Both projects were complete or largely complete. Evidence expansion was limited, no comparison condition was used, and one negative-item interpretation remains uncertain.

Usability problems were not observed in these two sessions. Not applicable was used in 9 of 12 sections, and evidence was expanded in only 2 of 12. Content coverage and evidence absence affected usefulness. Completed projects created a temporal distinction between documented past state and participant-recognised current state. **Further Round 2 evidence is required.**
