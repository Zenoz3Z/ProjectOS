Kaleidoscope — Research Notes

Research focus
Sterman et al. (2023) investigate how a documentation tool can make design processes visible and support reflection in a university user interface design course. Kaleidoscope brings project materials and related feedback into shared studio spaces (PDF pp. 3, 8–10).

Method
The researchers used Research through Design and iteratively developed the tool during a four-month course deployment. Their evaluation drew on interviews, surveys, reflection assignments, critique sessions and staff discussions. Qualitative data were examined through thematic analysis (PDF pp. 6, 10–12).

Key findings
Project histories provided material for reflection, but recording work could interrupt creation. Visible histories could communicate progress while also becoming overwhelming. Sharing unfinished work raised privacy concerns, and early technical problems affected trust in the tool (PDF pp. 12–15).

Relevance to ProjectOS
These findings suggest that ProjectOS should make earlier materials useful during ongoing work while keeping documentation manageable. I propose exploring whether source-linked retrieval can help users revisit previous decisions. This is a design hypothesis, rather than a result established by Kaleidoscope.

Limitations
The study concerns a specific educational setting and does not evaluate RAG. Its findings should not be treated as causal evidence of improved creativity. The authors also report limitations in interaction logging (PDF p. 18).

Sterman, S., Nicholas, M.J., Vivrekar, J., Mindel, J.R. and Paulos, E. (2023) ‘Kaleidoscope: A Reflective Documentation Tool for a User Interface Design Course’, Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems.
