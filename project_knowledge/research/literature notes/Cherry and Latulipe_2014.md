Reading Notes — Cherry and Latulipe (2014)

Reference

Cherry, E., and Latulipe, C. (2014). Quantifying the Creativity Support of Digital Tools through the Creativity Support Index. ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, 21(4), Article 21, 1–25. https://doi.org/10.1145/2617588

Research focus

The paper presents the Creativity Support Index (CSI), a psychometric survey for evaluating how users perceive the support provided by a tool during a specific creative activity. It measures creativity support rather than the objective creativity or quality of an artifact.

Structure

The CSI contains twelve agreement items across six factors: Exploration, Expressiveness, Immersion, Enjoyment, Results Worth Effort, and Collaboration. Fifteen paired-factor comparisons establish task-specific weights, which are combined with the item ratings to produce an overall score out of 100.

Development and validation

The CSI was developed iteratively through literature synthesis, vocabulary research, factor analysis, item selection, and studies involving different creative tools. However, reliability varied across factors and the initial Collaboration scale required revision.

Relevance to ProjectOS

CSI could provide structured feedback after users complete a defined project re-entry task using their own materials. Exploration and Results Worth Effort are particularly relevant to revisiting decisions and balancing reflective value against documentation effort. The complete CSI should be supplemented with interviews, task observations, and RAG reliability tests.

Critical limitations

CSI scores depend on the tool, task, and participants’ expertise. The measure is self-reported and does not demonstrate improved creativity or long-term project outcomes. Its concept of immersion may also conflict with reflective AI interactions that require users to remain aware of sources and system limitations.
