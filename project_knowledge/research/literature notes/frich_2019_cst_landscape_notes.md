Research focus
Frich et al. (2019) review creativity support tools in HCI to clarify their characteristics and support comparison across studies. The review covers 143 papers from the ACM Digital Library published between 1999 and 2018 (PDF pp. 1–2).

Method
The authors focus on researcher-developed tools. Sampling uses citation-based and download-based thresholds for different publication periods. Three researchers developed and refined a coding scheme covering tool characteristics, users, creative activities, evaluation and collaboration (PDF pp. 4–5).

Relevant findings
The review considers creativity support across activities including ideation, implementation, evaluation, iteration and project management. Ideation and implementation were more commonly represented than iteration and process management. The authors also discuss limited tool availability and the distance between research prototypes and professional practice (PDF pp. 7–10).

Implications for ProjectOS
This review supports investigating creative activities beyond idea generation. ProjectOS could focus on revisiting project materials, understanding previous decisions and planning subsequent iterations. Its contribution should be explained through these activities and evaluated in a specific user context.

Critical assessment
The sample does not represent all creativity support tools or commercial products. Citation and download thresholds may introduce selection bias, and the review ends in 2018. It therefore cannot establish a current research gap by itself. The authors’ CST definition is tentative, and their evaluation discussion supports selecting methods appropriate to the particular tool and research question (PDF pp. 4–5, 9–10).

Frich, J., MacDonald Vermeulen, L., Remy, C., Biskjaer, M.M. and Dalsgaard, P. (2019) ‘Mapping the Landscape of Creativity Support Tools in HCI’, Proceedings of the 2019 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, Paper 389, pp. 1–18.
