## Status

This evaluation plan is provisional.

Participant testing will not begin until the research scope, study design and evaluation priorities have been discussed with the supervisor.

## Research question

**How can a source-grounded and correctable Project Re-entry Brief support creative students in reconstructing project state and deciding what to do next after an interruption?**

## Evaluation purpose

The evaluation should investigate the interaction and research question rather than only checking whether the software runs.

The study should examine whether users can:

- understand the six-section Re-entry Brief;
- inspect the evidence behind a reconstruction;
- recognise inaccurate or outdated project states;
- correct or reject misleading reconstruction;
- distinguish current and superseded directions;
- and identify a reasonable next action.

The evaluation should also identify whether reviewing and correcting the system creates too much additional effort.

## Main evaluation questions

### EQ1 — Project-state clarity

Does the Re-entry Brief help users form a clear understanding of the project’s current state?

### EQ2 — Evidence use

Do users inspect and use the visible source evidence when judging the reconstructed state?

### EQ3 — Correction and agency

Can users recognise, correct or reject inaccurate reconstruction without losing access to the original evidence?

### EQ4 — Re-entry readiness

After using ProjectOS, do users feel ready to explain the current project direction and decide what to do next?

### EQ5 — Interaction cost

Does reviewing and correcting the system create unnecessary cognitive or practical effort?

## Recommended participant scope

The complete study should involve approximately five to eight adult participants.

Participants should be:

- creative students;
- recent creative graduates;
- or creative practitioners with experience of projects lasting several weeks or longer.

Participants do not need specialist knowledge of RAG or machine learning.

A manageable two-stage structure would be:

### Iteration 1

Three formative participants.

Purpose:

- identify interface problems;
- test whether section labels are understandable;
- observe whether users open the evidence;
- and identify problems with Confirm, Correct and Reject.

### Iteration 2

Five new participants after prototype revision.

Purpose:

- evaluate the revised interaction;
- collect comparable task results;
- and conduct short reflective interviews.

This would produce a maximum of eight participants across both iterations.

## Recommended study material

### Primary material — Prepared archive

All participants should first use the same prepared project archive.

The archive should contain:

- a clear current project aim;
- at least one previous experiment;
- one documented design change;
- tutor or user feedback;
- one resolved issue;
- one unresolved blocker;
- one superseded direction;
- and one documented next action.

Using a common archive allows participant responses to be compared against the same documented project state.

The Echo Field archive can be refined for this purpose.

### Secondary material — Participant project discussion

After completing the common task, participants may be asked to discuss how the interaction would apply to one of their own projects.

Using personal project files should remain optional unless the supervisor confirms that it is necessary and ethically appropriate.

This secondary discussion can provide ecological insight without making every participant’s primary task structurally different.

## Proposed participant task

### Part 1 — Context

Tell the participant that they are returning to a creative project after an interruption.

Explain that the project archive contains notes from different stages and may include outdated or resolved information.

Do not explain which information is current.

### Part 2 — Inspect the Re-entry Brief

Ask the participant to inspect all six sections.

They may open any evidence items they consider relevant.

### Part 3 — Review the reconstruction

Ask the participant to use:

- Confirm;
- Correct;
- or Reject

for selected sections.

If they believe an evidence item describes an old direction, ask them to explain why.

### Part 4 — Reconstruct the project state

Ask the participant to answer:

1. What is the project currently trying to achieve?
2. What important change was made?
3. Which direction is no longer current?
4. What remains unresolved?
5. What should happen next?

### Part 5 — Reflection

Ask the participant to explain:

- which section was most useful;
- which section was confusing;
- whether they trusted the reconstruction;
- whether the evidence changed their judgement;
- and whether correcting the system felt useful or burdensome.

## Data to collect

### Interaction data

Record:

- sections opened;
- evidence items opened;
- Confirm actions;
- Correct actions;
- Reject actions;
- correction text;
- and time required to complete the task.

Interaction logs should not be treated as evidence of success without participant explanation.

### Task responses

Record the participant’s answers about:

- current intention;
- important changes;
- superseded direction;
- unresolved blocker;
- and next action.

Responses can be compared with the documented state of the prepared archive.

### Short rating questions

After the task, participants can rate the following from 1 to 5:

1. The brief helped me understand the current project state.
2. The source evidence helped me judge the reconstruction.
3. I understood why the system selected the evidence.
4. I felt able to correct the system when it was misleading.
5. The review process required reasonable effort.
6. I would feel ready to resume work on the project.

These ratings should support rather than replace the qualitative findings.

### Interview data

Use a short semi-structured interview to collect:

- reasons for confirmation or correction;
- trust and uncertainty;
- perceived control;
- missing information;
- interface problems;
- and suggestions for the next iteration.

## Proposed analysis

### Quantitative description

Report simple descriptive results such as:

- number of Confirm, Correct and Reject actions;
- number of evidence items opened;
- completion time;
- and median or range of rating responses.

The sample is too small for strong statistical claims.

### Qualitative analysis

Use lightweight thematic analysis to identify patterns such as:

- evidence improved confidence;
- outdated evidence caused confusion;
- section labels were unclear;
- correction increased agency;
- correction required too much effort;
- or next-action support was insufficient.

Participant quotations should be anonymised.

## Comparison question

A conventional folder-review or AI-summary comparison could strengthen the project, but it would also increase study complexity.

A formal comparison should only be used if the supervisor considers it necessary.

For the current timeframe, a task-based formative study may provide stronger evidence than an underpowered experimental comparison.

If a comparison is required, the design should avoid presenting two conditions in a fixed order because participants may learn the project archive during the first condition.

Possible solutions include:

- two matched project archives;
- counterbalanced condition order;
- or separate participant groups.

These options should be discussed before implementation.

## Relationship to the Creativity Support Index

The Creativity Support Index may provide useful dimensions such as exploration, expressiveness, immersion, enjoyment and collaboration.

However, ProjectOS currently focuses on project re-entry rather than creative production during the session.

The full CSI should not be used automatically.

It should only be included if the supervisor agrees that its constructs match the final interaction and it can be administered correctly.

## Provisional success indicators

The prototype would show promising support if participants can:

- explain the project’s current intention using evidence;
- identify at least one superseded direction;
- recognise the seeded resolved-versus-unresolved distinction;
- correct a misleading section;
- understand that similarity is not confidence;
- and identify a documented next action.

These indicators should be refined after the formative study rather than converted into arbitrary thresholds in advance.

## Ethical and practical boundaries

The study should:

- involve consenting adults;
- avoid sensitive personal project material;
- allow participants to withdraw;
- anonymise interview data;
- store only necessary interaction records;
- avoid uploading participant documents to external services;
- and explain that the prototype may produce misleading reconstruction.

The current local retrieval pipeline is appropriate because project documents and review history remain on the local machine.

6. and whether the evaluation questions align with the research question.
